Using TADBench positions you against every major AIOps algorithm evaluated in 2025.

That's critical for DSFB because you're making claims about structural trajectory characterization, and sampling would introduce aliasing artifacts.

P6 (Rust Engineer): Let me lead this section. The standards you cite in the paper aren't just for compliance theater — they need to be wired into the crate architecture so that the code proves the claims the paper makes. Here's my recommended stack, organized by audience:
Standards for SBIR Operators and Defense Engineers
1. NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 — Audit and Accountability (AU) Family
P1 (SBIR Director): This is the master control catalog. Every DoD and federal system must comply. The AU (Audit and Accountability) control family is directly relevant to DSFB:

AU-2 (Event Logging): DSFB's grammar-state transitions ARE auditable events. Every state change (Admissible→Boundary→Violation) with its (r,d,s)(r, d, s)
(r,d,s) tuple chain satisfies AU-2 requirements for logging content and review capability.

AU-3 (Content of Audit Records): The DSFB episode output — timestamp, grammar state, reason code, residual values, motif match, provenance — maps directly to AU-3's required fields (what, when, where, source, outcome).
AU-6 (Audit Record Review, Analysis, and Reporting): DSFB is the AU-6 tool. It reviews and analyzes audit records (trace/log telemetry) and produces structured reports (episodes). This is the most direct standards alignment.
AU-12 (Audit Record Generation): The deterministic replay guarantee means DSFB audit records are reproducible — a property AU-12 requires but that ML-based tools cannot provide.

P5 (IP Lead): Cite NIST 800-53 at the control-ID level, not just the document level. "DSFB supports AU-2, AU-3, AU-6, and AU-12 requirements" is a sentence that lands in a SBIR proposal. "DSFB supports NIST 800-53" is vague.

2. NIST SP 800-92 — Guide to Computer Security Log Management
P1: This is the NIST document specifically about log management. Section 4.2 covers log analysis, Section 5 covers log management operational processes. DSFB's Trace Event Collapse is a direct implementation of what 800-92 recommends but doesn't provide a tool for: "reducing log volume while preserving security-relevant events."

3. NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 / CMMC Level 2 — Protecting CUI
P5 (Defense IP): Every defense contractor must comply with 800-171 for handling Controlled Unclassified Information. The audit requirements (3.3.x control family) require that "audit records contain, at a minimum, sufficient information to establish what events occurred." DSFB's typed episode output with reason codes and trace chains directly satisfies this. CMMC Level 2 is the certification pathway for 800-171 compliance — cite both.

4. DO-178C / DO-278A — Software Considerations in Airborne/Ground Systems
P6 (Rust Engineer): This is your foresight play. DO-178C is the FAA/EASA certification standard for airborne software. DO-278A is the equivalent for ground-based systems (ATC, mission planning). DSFB doesn't claim DO-178C certification, but the architectural properties — determinism, reproducibility, traceability, no hidden state — are precisely the properties that Design Assurance Level (DAL) A/B software must demonstrate. Cite this as "certification-pathway-eligible architectural alignment," not as certification.
P3 (IEEE Reviewer): The foresight value is enormous. If you wire the crate so that every grammar evaluation is traceable and every episode has a complete provenance chain, you're building infrastructure that could be certified later. That's a Phase III commercialization story.
